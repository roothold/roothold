Introducing RootHold

Why we're building an economic-development platform around a single idea — community.

FirstLight Editorial June 18, 20266 min read

Most development begins with a building. RootHold begins with a question almost no one asks: what would it take to build something a region organizes its whole life around — and keeps for a generation?

For decades, economic development has run on one-off deals. A company is courted, an incentive is signed, a warehouse goes up. Jobs arrive, and sometimes they leave. What's left behind is real estate — not an economy, and rarely a community. We think that model is exhausted. RootHold is our answer to it.

We are an economic-development architect. We design mixed-use, Foreign-Trade Zone campuses — roughly forty acres each — that import goods, make and assemble products, hold them in shared cold storage, and distribute them across a region. Then we fill those campuses with operating companies that make them run. One blueprint, built to be chosen, repeated coast to coast.

We don't build real estate. We build the anchor a region grows around — and the belonging that keeps it there.
The RootHold Thesis

A platform, not a project

RootHold already operates four companies: Empire Building Materials, Surplus Pods, Sweetkiwi, and Qulana. Building materials, autonomous retail, food, and beauty. They are proof that we don't just develop the real estate — we operate what goes inside it. Few developers can do both. That combination is the product.

Each campus is engineered to serve the industries that need all of it at once: food and beverage, health, beauty, pharmaceuticals, building materials, e-commerce, and advanced manufacturing. A shared cold chain runs through the middle — one temperature-controlled asset the whole platform, and a roster of paying tenants, can use.

Belonging is the business model

Here is the part that makes RootHold different, and durable. An anchor draws a region in — jobs cluster, property values rise, companies co-locate to be near it. But belonging is what keeps them. Membership and community, not one-off deals, create recurring revenue and retention that compound across the entire platform. A community you belong to beats a place you pass through. That is the moat.

It's also the goal. We're building toward a national network of operating companies, each generating a million dollars or more in annual recurring revenue — one hundred by 2030, three hundred by 2036 — anchored by campuses and a network of automated retail Surplus Pods. Belonging that compounds into a platform no single location could build alone.

RootHold is a platform you can join — as a founder growing inside the network, a city looking for an anchor worth competing for, or a partner backing durable assets that compound. Community is the DNA of every partnership we make. We've built the system. Now we build with you.
