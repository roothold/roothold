Introducing Surplus Pods

The autonomous, 24/7 store that lives inside the buildings where people already are.

FirstLight Editorial July 2, 20265 min read

Every building has square footage no one uses well — a quiet lobby, a forgotten corner, a floor that empties at five. Surplus Pods turn that dead space into the most convenient store its residents have ever had, open every hour of every day, run entirely on its own.

A Surplus Pod is a self-contained, AI-run retail environment that drops into the places people already live and work: residential lobbies, workforce housing, campuses, transit hubs. Walk in, take what you need, walk out. The Pod handles the rest — inventory, checkout, restocking signals, and merchandising tuned to who actually shops there.

Retail shouldn't ask people to go somewhere. It should already be where they are — the last mile, built in.
Surplus Pods

An amenity and a revenue stream

For a building owner, a Pod is two things at once. It's a premium amenity that makes a property more livable and more leasable — the kind of convenience residents notice and remember. And it's a new line of revenue from space that was earning nothing. No staffing to manage, no operating headache; RootHold runs the Pod end to end.

Built for the network

Individually, a Pod is a great store. Together, they're infrastructure. Anchored to a RootHold campus, a fleet of Pods turns a region into last-mile distribution across a 250-mile radius — and gives the platform's own brands, like Sweetkiwi, a direct shelf in every location. The shared cold chain on campus keeps refrigerated and frozen goods flowing to the Pods that need them.
